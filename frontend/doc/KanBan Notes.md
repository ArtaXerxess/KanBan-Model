# The KanBan Model

The Kanban model typically uses columns to represent the flow of tasks through different stages of a workflow. Each column corresponds to a specific stage or category of work, and tasks (or "cards") move from one column to another as they progress. The specific headings of the columns can vary depending on the type of work being managed, but here are some common ones:

### 1. **Backlog / To Do**
   - This column contains tasks that are planned but haven't yet started. It’s essentially a queue of tasks that are waiting to be worked on.

### 2. **In Progress**
   - Tasks in this column are actively being worked on. It represents the current work in progress (WIP).

### 3. **Review / Testing**
   - This stage holds tasks that have been completed but are waiting for review or testing before being finalized.

### 4. **Done**
   - This column contains tasks that are finished and have passed review or testing. These tasks are considered complete.

### Optional Columns
Depending on your workflow, you might also include additional columns to represent intermediate steps:

- **Ready / Selected for Development**: Tasks that have been reviewed and are ready to start.
- **Blocked**: Tasks that are impeded by some issue and cannot progress.
- **Deployment**: If you have a specific deployment or release process.
- **On Hold**: Tasks that are paused temporarily and will resume later.

### Example Kanban Columns:

1. **Backlog / To Do**
2. **In Progress**
3. **Review / Testing**
4. **Done**

The flow of tasks moves left to right: from "Backlog" to "In Progress," then to "Review/Testing," and finally to "Done."

---

### Customizing Columns

In your application, you can adjust these stages to fit your specific needs or workflow. For instance, in a software development process, you might have additional stages like **Code Review** or **Ready for Release**.
